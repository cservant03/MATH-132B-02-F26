    Subsection Course Description

    In this introduction to statistical methodology, students identify methods of acquiring, organizing and analyzing data so as to make decisions and respond to arguments. Topics include graphical representation of data, probability distributions, confidence intervals and hypothesis test, parametric and non-parametric techniques, regression and correlation, and analysis of variance. Special emphasis on applications from biology and the health sciences.

    Subsection Evaluatation

    Video Notes
    🔗
    For each section covered in class, students are required to submit completed notes based on the assigned lecture videos. One submission is required per section and is due before the start of class on the day that section is covered. Notes are graded on a 0–2 scale:
    🔗
    2:
    🔗
    Notes are submitted on time, are complete, and reflect engagement with the assigned videos.
    🔗
    1:
    🔗
    Notes are submitted on time but are missing significant portions.
    🔗
    0:
    🔗
    Notes are not submitted on time.
    🔗
    Notes are graded for completion only, not for correctness. No late submissions are accepted. Notes may be handwritten, typed, or completed on a tablet, but all submissions must be uploaded as a single PDF file. Students are expected to know how to scan or create a PDF of their notes; guidance will be provided as needed.
    🔗
    Worksheets
    🔗
    Worksheets are completed during class and are intended to help students practice and apply the concepts being studied. Each worksheet is paired with a MyOpenMath assignment. Students are expected to answer the questions in MyOpenMath and show supporting work on the worksheet packet. Both the completed worksheet and completion of the associated MyOpenMath assignment are required for credit.
    🔗
    Students may collaborate with classmates while working on worksheets and may use their notes and classroom laptops. The use of cell phones, generative AI tools, or other unauthorized resources is not permitted. Worksheets are due at the end of class and may be submitted up until the beginning of the next class period without penalty. Worksheets submitted after the start of the next class period will not be accepted.
    🔗
    While correctness is primarily assessed through MyOpenMath, students are expected to show clear and meaningful work on their worksheet packets. Work that is incomplete, inconsistent with the submitted answers, or does not demonstrate an attempt to solve the problem may result in reduced credit.
    🔗
    Quizzes
    🔗
    Quizzes are administered approximately every fourth class meeting throughout most of the semester. Quizzes assess a student’s ability to independently apply the concepts and skills developed through class activities, worksheets, and assigned course materials.
    🔗
    Each quiz is scored on a 0-3 scale:
    🔗
    3:
    🔗
    Demonstrates a strong understanding of the outcome with little to no error. Work is complete, mathematically correct, and clearly communicates the solution process.
    🔗
    2:
    🔗
    Demonstrates substantial understanding of the outcome but contains one or more minor errors. The overall solution process is appropriate and most of the work is correct.
    🔗
    1:
    🔗
    Demonstrates partial understanding of the outcome but contains significant errors or omissions. Some correct work is shown, but the student has not yet demonstrated competency.
    🔗
    0:
    🔗
    The outcome is not attempted or the work submitted does not demonstrate an understanding of the outcome.
    🔗
    Each quiz includes two in-class attempts. For each quiz, the highest score earned across all attempts is used when calculating the Quiz portion of the course grade.
    🔗
    A third attempt is available only after attending tutoring. Students must work with a tutor to review the relevant worksheet problems and their first two attempts before a third attempt will be granted.
    🔗
    Quizzes are not given as make-ups. The multiple-attempt structure is intended to accommodate absences and scheduling conflicts. Students who miss an in-class attempt may use a remaining attempt to demonstrate competency.
    🔗
    For the final quizzes of the course, only one in-class attempt may be provided during the final exam period. Additional attempts must be completed outside of class time by appointment.

    Lab Reports
    🔗
    Students will complete three lab reports during the semester. Each report requires students to locate an appropriate data set, analyze the data using the statistical methods studied in the course, and communicate their findings clearly. The source of the data set must be identified in the report.
    🔗
    The three lab reports have the following data requirements:
    1. Inference for Numerical Data.
      The data set must contain a quantitative response variable and a categorical explanatory variable with at least three groups. There must be enough observations in each group to perform an ANOVA and evaluate the conditions required for the analysis.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Inference for Categorical Data.
      The data set must contain at least two categorical variables. Each variable must have at least two categories so that the data can be organized into a contingency table of at least size \(2\times 2\text{.}\) There must be enough observations to perform an appropriate chi-square test.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Regression.
      The data set must contain at least two quantitative variables with paired observations. There must be enough observations to construct and analyze a regression model.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Each lab report must contain the following components:
    1. Data Set Summary.
      In no more than one page, describe the source and context of the data, identify the variables being studied, explain how the data were collected when that information is available, and state the question or questions being investigated.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Graphical Analysis.
      Construct appropriate statistical graphics that help describe the data and address the questions being investigated. All graphics must include appropriate titles, labels, units, and legends when needed.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Statistical Analysis.
      Perform the required statistical procedures and several appropriate statistical tests on the data. Include the relevant calculations, verify the conditions or assumptions for each procedure, and report the results using appropriate statistical notation.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Summary of Results.
      Explain the results of the statistical procedures in the context of the data. The discussion should identify the important findings and explain what the numerical results, confidence intervals, significance tests, or models indicate.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Conclusion.
      In no more than one page, summarize the principal findings, answer the questions posed at the beginning of the report, and discuss any important limitations of the data or analysis.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Each lab report is worth 50 points and will be evaluated using the following rubric.

    Subsection Grading

    Your final course grade will be determined by your performance in four categories: Video Notes, Worksheets, Quizzes, and Lab Reports. A letter grade will be determined separately for each category using the requirements below. Your final course grade will be the lowest of the four category grades.
    🔗
    Letter Grade Video Notes Completed Worksheets Completed Quiz Points Lab Report Points
    A 90%–100% 90%–100% 40-45 77-90
    B 80% to less than 90% 80% to less than 90% 33-39 63-76
    C 70% to less than 80% 70% to less than 80% 27-32 50-62
    D 60% to less than 70% 60% to less than 70% 18-26 36-49
    F Less than 60% Less than 60% 0-17 0-35
    Here are some examples showing how final course grades are determined:
    🔗
    1. A student completes 94% of the Video Notes, completes 92% of the Worksheets, earns 56 Quiz points, and earns 140 Lab Report points. The student earns an A in all four categories. Therefore, the student’s final course grade is an A.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. A student completes 95% of the Video Notes, completes 84% of the Worksheets, earns 55 Quiz points, and earns 135 Lab Report points. The student earns an A for Video Notes, a B for Worksheets, an A for Quizzes, and an A for Lab Reports. Since the lowest category grade is a B, the student’s final course grade is a B.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. A student completes 83% of the Video Notes, completes 84% of the Worksheets, earns 51 Quiz points, and earns 100 Lab Report points. The student earns a B for Video Notes, a B for Worksheets, a B for Quizzes, and a C for Lab Reports. Since the lowest category grade is a C, the student’s final course grade is a C.
      🔗
      🔗
    Plus and minus grades may be assigned based on a student’s overall participation and effort throughout the semester, including class participation and attendance at office hours or tutoring sessions. For example, a student whose category grade is a B but who demonstrates limited participation and effort may receive a B-, while a student whose category grade is a C and who demonstrates a high level of participation and effort may receive a C+. A student who demonstrates the expected level of participation and effort will receive the unmodified letter grade. SVSU does not award C-, D+, or D- grades, so these grades will not be assigned.
