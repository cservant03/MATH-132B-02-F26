    Example 32. Pollinator Survey.

    Biologists observed 180 insects visiting flowers in a restored meadow. For each insect, they recorded the type of insect and whether visible pollen was present on its body.
    🔗
    1. Let \(B\) be the event that a randomly selected insect is a bumblebee and let \(F\) be the event that it is a butterfly. Are \(B\) and \(F\) mutually exclusive? Explain.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the probability that a randomly selected insect is a bumblebee or a butterfly. Use probability notation in your answer.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Let \(E\) be the event that a randomly selected insect is a bee (a bumblebee or honeybee) and let \(P\) be the event that visible pollen is present. Are \(E\) and \(P\) mutually exclusive? Explain.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Find the probability that a randomly selected insect is a bee or has visible pollen. Use probability notation in your answer.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Example 34. West Nile Virus in Mosquito Pools.

    A public-health laboratory tests one mosquito pool from each of two distant wetlands. The test results from the two wetlands are independent. The probability that the pool from the first wetland tests positive for West Nile virus is \(0.08\text{,}\) and the probability that the pool from the second wetland tests positive is \(0.12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    1. What is the probability that both mosquito pools test positive?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. What is the probability that both mosquito pools test negative?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. What is the probability that the two mosquito pools have the same test result? Use probability notation and the addition rule in your answer.
      🔗
      🔗

    Example 38. Seal Health Assessments.

    Veterinarians examined 150 seals at a marine-animal rehabilitation center. They recorded whether each seal was underweight and whether it had a respiratory infection.
    🔗
    1. Are the events “underweight” and “respiratory infection present” mutually exclusive? Explain.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the probability that a randomly selected seal is underweight or has a respiratory infection. Use probability notation in your answer.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Are the events “underweight” and “not underweight” mutually exclusive? Explain.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Find the probability that a randomly selected seal is underweight or is not underweight. Explain why your answer makes sense.
