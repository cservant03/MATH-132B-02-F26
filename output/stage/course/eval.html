    Subsection Evaluatation

    Homework
    🔗
    Each learning outcome in the course has one 10-point homework assignment in MyOpenMath. These assignments provide practice applying the concepts and skills associated with each outcome and help students prepare for quizzes and lab reports. A student’s Homework score is the total number of points earned across all MyOpenMath homework assignments.
    🔗
    Quizzes
    🔗
    Quizzes are administered approximately every fourth class meeting throughout most of the semester. Quizzes assess a student’s ability to independently apply the concepts and skills developed through class notes and assigned course materials.
    🔗
    Each quiz is scored on a 0-3 scale:
    🔗
    3:
    🔗
    Demonstrates a strong understanding of the outcome with little to no error. Work is complete, mathematically correct, and clearly communicates the solution process.
    🔗
    2:
    🔗
    Demonstrates substantial understanding of the outcome but contains one or more minor errors. The overall solution process is appropriate and most of the work is correct.
    🔗
    1:
    🔗
    Demonstrates partial understanding of the outcome but contains significant errors or omissions. Some correct work is shown, but the student has not yet demonstrated competency.
    🔗
    0:
    🔗
    The outcome is not attempted or the work submitted does not demonstrate an understanding of the outcome.
    🔗
    Each quiz includes two in-class attempts. For each quiz, the highest score earned across all attempts is used when calculating the Quiz portion of the course grade.
    🔗
    A third attempt is available only after attending tutoring. Students must work with a tutor to review the relevant worksheet problems and their first two attempts before a third attempt will be granted.
    🔗
    Quizzes are not given as make-ups. The multiple-attempt structure is intended to accommodate absences and scheduling conflicts. Students who miss an in-class attempt may use a remaining attempt to demonstrate competency.
    🔗
    For the final quizzes of the course, only one in-class attempt may be provided during the final exam period. Additional attempts must be completed outside of class time by appointment.
    🔗
    Lab Reports
    🔗
    Students will complete three lab reports during the semester. Each report requires students to locate an appropriate data set, analyze the data using the statistical methods studied in the course, and communicate their findings clearly. The source of the data set must be identified in the report.
    🔗
    The three lab reports have the following data requirements:
    1. Inference for Numerical Data.
      The data set must contain a quantitative response variable and a categorical explanatory variable with at least three groups. There must be enough observations in each group to perform an ANOVA and evaluate the conditions required for the analysis.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Inference for Categorical Data.
      The data set must contain at least two categorical variables. Each variable must have at least two categories so that the data can be organized into a contingency table of at least size \(2\times 2\text{.}\) There must be enough observations to perform an appropriate chi-square test.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Regression.
      The data set must contain at least two quantitative variables with paired observations. There must be enough observations to construct and analyze a regression model.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Each lab report must contain the following components:
    1. Data Set Summary.
      In no more than one page, describe the source and context of the data, identify the variables being studied, explain how the data were collected when that information is available, and state the question or questions being investigated.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Graphical Analysis.
      Construct appropriate statistical graphics that help describe the data and address the questions being investigated. All graphics must include appropriate titles, labels, units, and legends when needed.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Statistical Analysis.
      Perform the required statistical procedures and several appropriate statistical tests on the data. Include the relevant calculations, verify the conditions or assumptions for each procedure, and report the results using appropriate statistical notation.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Summary of Results.
      Explain the results of the statistical procedures in the context of the data. The discussion should identify the important findings and explain what the numerical results, confidence intervals, significance tests, or models indicate.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Conclusion.
      In no more than one page, summarize the principal findings, answer the questions posed at the beginning of the report, and discuss any important limitations of the data or analysis.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Each lab report is worth 50 points and will be evaluated using the following rubric.
